**Paper can be damaged, and `paperlab/fx` is what damages it.**

`<Paper damage={source}>` is a new prop on the main entry: a grid of char, saturation, heat and missing paper over the sheet's UV, which the sheet draws and simulates without knowing what caused it — the same split `content` already has. `DamageSource` is the whole seam (see `DAMAGE_CHANNELS`), so a baked texture or a recorded burn played back works as well as a live simulation. An untouched field draws byte-identical to no field at all, which a render, not an argument, is what settles.

What the sheet does with it:

- **Shading** — a brown scorch going black, wet paper darker and smoother, holes cut at half presence (in the shadow map too, through a custom depth material), and a per-fragment fray that makes a burnt edge ragged rather than a staircase. How ragged is `DamageSource.detail`, which a source can turn down.
- **Physics**, on a cloth sheet — char shrinks the paper and curls it toward the flame, saturation makes it heavier, and paper that has burnt away leaves the solve, so a sheet whose lower half is gone stops hanging off the weight of nothing. A hand cannot take hold of paper that is not there.
- **`PaperHandle.surfacePoint(u, v)`** answers where a point of the sheet is in world space this frame, on the drawn surface — which is how anything can emit from a sheet that is draped, blown or crumpled.

`paperlab/fx` is a new subpath, and it holds the causes: `DamageField` (a CPU, untiered, fixed-timestep simulation of heat, water, char and presence, with the paper's own grain and fibre direction in it), the particle pool with ember, smoke and ash presets, `FireEmitter`, `FxParticles` to draw them, `FxPost` for the glow — bloom over the HDR frame with the rig's tone curve put back last, lazy-loaded so its two peers stay optional — and `FxAudio` + `FireSound`, which synthesises a fire from the same numbers the picture is drawn from — a bed on the length of the burn front, a crackle on the rate paper chars. Its own subpath so that a `<Paper>` consumer never resolves any of it.

**What a burn looks like** was rebuilt against a visual spec, and it is what this release ships:

- **The sheet** draws a burn in zones measured in millimetres from the cut — a pale, lifted ash lip; the **ember line**, beaded and alive on the burn's own clock and the only thing on the sheet that emits; cracked, blistered char; and a scorch with a steep leading edge that reaches further up than down. Heat paints nothing anywhere else — the wide warm band earlier builds drew over unburnt paper is gone. `DamageSource.time` is a new optional clock the ember line animates on, so a replayed burn flickers the same way twice.
- **`FxPost`** — bloom above paper white, heat haze over the flames and a warm grade, with the rig's tone curve put back last. Lazy, with `@react-three/postprocessing` and `postprocessing` as optional peers.
- **`FxFireFluid`** — the flames are a small GPU fire simulation on a camera-facing plane: fuel and premixed oxygen burn into heat and smoke, buoyancy and curl-noise turbulence move it, and a pressure solve keeps it swirling instead of smearing. It is fed from the hot rim in uneven clusters (`flameAnchors`), never a uniform crown. Its render pass reads a NORMALISED temperature, so a flame has a pale core, a saturated body and a torn tip rather than one saturated colour; a domain warp and a noise that bites only at the cool end give it detail finer than the grid, so tongues split instead of dissolving. `FireFluid` is the solver on its own; `fireFluidDefaults` and `fireFluidControls` are its parameters (emission, fuel, premixed and ambient oxygen, heat, smoke, radial impulse, velocity, burn rate, gas expansion, buoyancy, cooling, persistence, turbulence and its scale, vorticity, wind), and `fxQualityTiers` gains `fluid` for its grid sizes. Where half-float targets are missing it draws its `fallback` instead.
- **`DamageSource.look`** (`DamageLook`, defaults in `DAMAGE_LOOK_DEFAULTS`) sets how a burn is drawn — ember width, intensity, coverage, flicker, glow and sparkle; ash lip width and brightness; char warmth and cracks; scorch reach, darkness and fingers; and how much the edge waves and bites — read every frame, so it can be tuned live. The ember line now carries a crimson glow into the char beside it and flickering fibre specks, and the edge of a hole wanders instead of following the grid.
- **`FxFlames`** (sprite flames, now the fallback for `FxFireFluid`: world-up tongues on the hot rim, taller above than below), **`FxFireLight`** (the burn lights the sheet, and glows through it), **`FxMatchFlame`** (a match in a hand: arming sparks, a strike that flares, a held flame that leans and stretches) and **`FxWisps`** (the thread of smoke from a smouldering bead), with `flameAnchors` and `flamePuff` for anything that wants to stand where the flames stand.
- **`Afterglow`** keeps the embers a blown-out edge would have after its heat has gone, as a `DamageSource` over the field — presentation only; the physics reads what it always did.
- `FxParticles` draws embers as streaks, smoke as torn sprites and ash as tumbling planes; `ParticlePool.write` takes an optional flakes target and fills velocities, `takePops()` reports embers that flash in the air, and `FireEmitter` takes per-kind `caps`. `FireSound` gains `strike()`, `puff()` and `pop()`. `fxQualityTiers` gains `bloomScale`, `flames`, `caps` and `haze`.
- **`DamageField` burns at the speed paper burns.** Its four fire rates were all six times too fast — a front spreading at 39 mm/s against the 3–8 mm/s a sheet of A4 actually manages, so a sheet was three-quarters gone in under five seconds and there was no time to watch it spread. A burn front's speed goes as the square root of diffusion times reaction and the charred band behind it as their ratio, so `heatDiffusion`, `charRate` and `cooling` are divided by the same six: the fire slows and its shape is untouched. `consumeRate` comes back from 20 to 6 for the same reason it went up — the band is speed over that number, so 20 was holding it against a front six times too fast. Measured now: 6.8 mm/s from the centre, 3.6 from a corner, a 5.3 mm char band, a hole 22 mm across at 4 s, and a fire that lives about 11 s. `DamageField.time` is the field's simulated clock.

**How bright fire is, in one unit.** `paperlab/fx/emission` is new and it is the answer to the only quantitative claim the look makes: a glowing part of a fire is 4–8× brighter than the whitest paper in the frame. That claim used to be authored four different ways — sRGB hex in the sheet's ember ramp, a raw linear triple in the particle preset, a curve in the fluid's render pass, and one absolute constant as the bloom threshold — with nothing converting between them, so being half as bright as intended was invisible. Now every emitter is authored through `emit(hue, timesPaperWhite)` against `PAPER_WHITE`, `FX_BLOOM_THRESHOLD` is stated as a multiple of it, and a test holds each emitter to the spec's band. `FIRE_BODY`, `FIRE_CORE` and `FIRE_HEAT_SCALE` say what a flame is worth: a saturated body above paper and under the threshold, small cores well past it.

`FX_BLOOM_THRESHOLD` is exported from here rather than from the post pass, so a consumer writing their own emissive can read it without installing the pass's optional peers.

**The defaults are the tuned look.** Every default below was measured against the reference stills rather than guessed, and is what a burn looks like out of the box: `DAMAGE_LOOK_DEFAULTS` (an ember line inside §5's 0.3–1 mm, a broken matte ash lip, char at a third of full warmth so it reads as charcoal rather than cardboard, a scorch reaching 30 mm up and an uneven edge), `fireFluidDefaults` (turbulence doing the work instead of vorticity and a radial impulse, which together rolled the flames into mushroom caps; flame persistence off its floor; cooling that keeps forty emission points from merging into one plume), `FIRE_LIGHT_GAIN` 42, `FX_BLOOM` 0.55 over `FX_BLOOM_THRESHOLD` (2.25× paper white), heat haze on the `high` tier only, and `fireEmitterDefaults` — now exported — at 0.35 embers, 0 smoke and 0.12 ash.

Smoke is 0 on purpose: a burning sheet's smoke is `FxFireFluid`'s, made of the same fluid as the flames, and `FxWisps` carries the thread once they are out. There used to be three smoke systems running at once.

A stated limit: a cut narrower than a cloth cell separates the picture, not the paper. Splitting a sheet in two needs a second mesh, and this is not that.
